\abstract{
Static space--time visualizations compress each time step of a 2-D field into a 1-D column and stack the columns over time. Their 1-D order is often constrained by a hierarchy: merge tree maps keep every merge-tree subtree contiguous, and dendrogram-ordered pixel displays keep every cluster contiguous. When the hierarchy disagrees with space, every admissible layout must misplace some feature, yet existing quality measures describe the layout at hand rather than the error that no layout can avoid. We model such maps as hierarchy-constrained 1-D interval layouts and prove three kinds of results. First, the smallest achievable maximum deviation from a positional reference, the \emph{certificate}, has a closed form for a fixed order, separates into a hierarchy and a space component, and is computed exactly at display resolution by a pseudo-polynomial dynamic program; a width-free variant bounds every merge tree map, whatever its width model. Second, breaking the contiguity of a subtree distorts merge levels by at least half the gap between its merge value and its parent's for any scalar filling, and the best value-preserving filling of a given order has a closed form. Third, these closed forms yield the exact position--topology frontier of all value-preserving 1-D maps for small trees and a certified lower bound for large ones. On three real datasets from two domains, hierarchy-induced conflicts occur in 58--63\% of time steps, and persistence simplification that deletes a third of the features leaves many of them unresolved. In a geometric reader proxy, existing merge tree maps show the wrong direction of approach or separation in 21--34\% of clear cases; even drawing every feature exactly at its reference position still gives 5--14\%. A certificate-driven relaxation with a merge-gap threshold of 20\% of the value range lowers this to 6--16\% at a topological cost that it reports exactly per time step, and the orders it selects lie on the exact frontier in 152 of 155 relaxed time steps. The certificate also transfers to 1024-leaf dendrogram displays.
}

\keywords{Merge trees, hierarchy-constrained layout, time-varying scalar fields, static visualization, faithfulness, interleaving distance.}

\firstsection{Introduction}
\maketitle

Static summaries show the complete evolution of spatio-temporal data in one image and complement animation, which makes comparisons across time hard. Hovm\"oller diagrams~\cite{Hovmoller:1949}, dense pixel displays and MotionRugs~\cite{Keim:2000:PIX,Buchmueller:2019:MR}, 1-D projections~\cite{Franke:2021:1DP}, and merge tree maps~\cite{Koepp:2023:TMTM,STMTM:2026} all compress space to one axis and put time on the other. Readers then infer proximity and motion from vertical position: features drawn close are read as close, and converging tracks as approaching.

These displays share a second property: \emph{their 1-D order is constrained by a hierarchy}. Merge tree maps keep every merge-tree subtree contiguous, which keeps features intact and makes the merge tree readable~\cite{Koepp:2023:TMTM}; clustered heatmaps~\cite{Eisen:1998:CA} and hierarchical pixel orderings~\cite{Franke:2021:1DP} keep clusters contiguous; geophylogenies align tree-constrained orders with map sites~\cite{Klawitter:2025:GP}. Hierarchy and space need not agree. Two pressure lows may merge first because they share a trough while an unrelated low lies between them (\cref{fig:toy}). Every admissible layout must then misplace one of the three, and the reader cannot know which one, or by how much.

Abstract views are known to mislead~\cite{McNutt:2020:SVM,Wattenberg:2016:TSNE}, and projection quality is routinely measured~\cite{Venna:2001:NP,Aupetit:2007:VD}; ST-MTM reports stress and neighbourhood preservation~\cite{STMTM:2026}. Such measures describe the layout that was produced. None certifies the \emph{unavoidable} deviation from a positional reference, names the features that cause it, or states how much hierarchy must be given up to reduce it. The consequence is concrete: in a geometric reader proxy on ERA5 sea-level pressure (\cref{sec:rq2}), the vertical distance change of two lows on a temporal merge tree map has the opposite sign of their true 24-hour approach or separation in 33.7\% of clear cases, and 24.9\% on ST-MTM, whereas drawing every low exactly at its reference position errs in 12.7\%.

We therefore ask: \textbf{how faithful to a positional reference can a hierarchy-constrained 1-D layout be, and can its unfaithfulness be certified, located, and controlled?} Following the argument that quality must be defined before it can be measured and guaranteed~\cite{Meulemans:2026:AP}, we characterize what \emph{any} layout can achieve and use this to build, disclose, and relax layouts. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A positional certificate}: closed form for fixed orders, hierarchy/space decomposition, witness triples, an exact pseudo-polynomial dynamic program with a certified lower bound, and a width-free variant for every merge tree map (\cref{sec:cert}).
\item \textbf{The price of topology}: a filling-independent lower bound on the merge-level distortion caused by breaking contiguity, the optimal value-preserving filling in closed form, and the exact position--topology frontier with a scalable certified bound (\cref{sec:topo}).
\item \textbf{A certificate-driven method} that relaxes the merge tree only where the certificate shows a conflict and discloses certificate, conflicting features, and topological cost per time step (\cref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{A cross-domain characterization} on three real and two synthetic datasets: conflicts are frequent and only partly removed by simplification, they reverse motion readings well above exact projection, relaxation trades reversals against an exactly reported topological cost close to the exact frontier, and the certificate answers a design question for dendrogram displays (\cref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Static summaries of spatio-temporal data.}
Hovm\"oller diagrams slice or aggregate the orthogonal dimension. Space-filling curves~\cite{Franke:2021:1DP,Zhou:2021:DSF} and pixel-oriented techniques~\cite{Keim:2000:PIX} linearize space densely. MotionRugs~\cite{Buchmueller:2019:MR}, stable visual summaries~\cite{Wulms:2021:SVS}, MoReVis~\cite{Valdrighi:2024:MRV}, and GroupRugs~\cite{Stolk:2025:GR} summarize trajectories and regions; GroupRugs keeps groups contiguous and measures spatial quality, but gives no bound on the conflict between grouping and space. Topological landscapes~\cite{Weber:2007:TL,Beketayev:2012:GPTL}, temporal treemaps and nested tracking graphs~\cite{Koepp:2019:TT,Lukasczyk:2017:NTG}, TMTM~\cite{Koepp:2023:TMTM}, and ST-MTM~\cite{STMTM:2026} lay out topology over time. TMTM orders subtrees to maximize the overlap of consecutive columns and allocates one pixel per sample; ST-MTM also places leaves to reflect geometric distances and fits leaf widths. Both preserve the merge tree exactly; neither controls or reports the deviation from a positional reference, which we certify.

\textbf{Hierarchy-constrained orders.}
Optimal leaf ordering~\cite{BarJoseph:2001:OLO} underlies clustered heatmaps~\cite{Eisen:1998:CA}. Tree-constrained orders against external positions appear in geophylogenies~\cite{Klawitter:2025:GP}, tree reordering~\cite{Bulteau:2022:RT}, and displacement-optimized tanglegrams~\cite{Huson:2026:DOT}, with crossing, inversion, or summed-displacement objectives; storyline layouts order lines under hierarchy constraints~\cite{Liu:2013:SF}. We address the bottleneck objective with widths, gaps, canvas, and eccentricity, and pair it with a topological cost. Without a hierarchy, the maximum displacement needed to separate intervals on a line is minimized in $O(n\log n)$~\cite{Li:2019:SOI}, the free case of our certificate; separation-constrained quadratic programs~\cite{Dwyer:2006:IPSEP} choose our layout within the certified budget.

\textbf{Faithfulness and distortion.}
Mirages~\cite{McNutt:2020:SVM} and misread embeddings~\cite{Wattenberg:2016:TSNE} show how abstract views mislead; trustworthiness~\cite{Venna:2001:NP} and distortion maps~\cite{Aupetit:2007:VD,Rauscher:2025:1DO,Rauscher:2026:VB} measure projection artefacts, and Nonato and Aupetit relate distortions to tasks~\cite{Nonato:2019:MP}. Cartograms~\cite{Nusrat:2016:CART}, data--spatial grid layouts~\cite{vanBeusekom:2023:DSL}, and spatially ordered treemaps~\cite{Wood:2008:SOT} trade or measure spatial consistency; visual--data correspondence~\cite{Kindlmann:2014:AVD} and graph faithfulness~\cite{Nguyen:2013:FGV} formalize what a view should preserve. These works assess produced layouts; we bound all layouts and compute the attainable trade-off.

\textbf{Topological simplification and merge tree distances.}
Persistence-based simplification removes low-persistence extrema~\cite{Edelsbrunner:2002:TPS,Carr:2004:SFI,Tierny:2012:GTS}; it can remove conflicts only by deleting features, whereas our flattening keeps every feature and removes a merge relation, at a cost $\gamma_v$ that is an adjacent merge gap, not a persistence (\cref{sec:rq1} compares them). Interleaving distances compare merge trees~\cite{Morozov:2013:IDMT}, the $\ell_\infty$-cophenetic metric of labelled trees is one~\cite{Munch:2019:LINF}, and intrinsic, geometry-aware, and Wasserstein variants support time-varying analysis~\cite{Gasparovic:2024:IID,Yan:2022:GAMT,Pont:2022:WMT}, including cyclones and atmospheric rivers~\cite{Yan:2024:TROPHY,Lan:2024:AR}. Persistent homology has evaluated and built projections~\cite{Rieck:2015:PH,Doraiswamy:2021:TM}, and the merge distortion metric compares cluster trees~\cite{Eldridge:2015:MD}. We use the labelled interleaving distance to measure what a 1-D map does to the merge tree it depicts.

\subsection{Merge tree maps}
For each time step $t$ of a scalar field $f_t$ on a 2-D domain, the merge tree $T_t$ records how the components of sublevel sets (a \emph{join tree}, e.g., for pressure minima) or superlevel sets (a \emph{split tree}, e.g., for fire-intensity maxima) are born at extrema and merge at saddles. Its leaves are the features of interest. Leaf $i$ has an extremum position $R_i\in\mathbb R^2$ and a support measure $A_i$ (the area of its segment), and every pair of leaves has a merge value $f(\lca(i,j))$. A merge tree map chooses, per time step, a leaf order, an interval per leaf, and an anchor inside the interval; fills the column by sampling values along leaf arcs and lowest-common-ancestor (LCA) paths~\cite{Koepp:2023:TMTM,STMTM:2026}; and stacks the columns over time. Because every subtree occupies a contiguous block, the merge levels read from the column equal those of $T_t$: the map preserves topology.

\subsection{Tasks and the role of the reference}\label{sec:tasks}
Readers perform three tasks on a merge tree map: (T1)~identify and compare features and how they merge; (T2)~locate a feature relative to others along a spatial direction; (T3)~judge whether two features approach or separate. T1 needs topology and size; T2 and T3 need position. Merge tree maps were designed for T1, but T2 and T3 are performed on them too: vertical proximity is the only spatial cue, ST-MTM places leaves to reflect geometric distances~\cite{STMTM:2026}, and 1-D projections are used to follow spatial phenomena~\cite{Franke:2021:1DP}.

\textbf{The reference.} We make the positional reading explicit through a \emph{reference coordinate} $q_i=\varphi(R_i)$, the 1-D coordinate of feature $i$ that a reader should recover from its vertical position. It belongs to the task, not to a method, and our results hold for any given reference; we use the principal direction of feature motion (T3) and report fixed directions for robustness. The certificate does not claim that TMTM or ST-MTM are wrong: it states what \emph{any} merge-tree-preserving map can offer a reader who performs T2 or T3 with that reference.

\textbf{Why the maximum.} A reader does not know which feature is misplaced, so a guarantee must hold for every feature; averages and stress hide the few misplaced features on which readings reverse (\cref{sec:rq2}); and the maximum admits a closed form, an exact algorithm, and a clean interaction with topology. Average measures still choose among layouts within the certified budget (\cref{sec:method}).

\subsection{A natural remedy, and why it is not enough}
The natural remedy asks every anchor $u_i$ to stay close to $q_i$: a linear program over all hierarchy-consistent orders finds the smallest maximum deviation, and a quadratic program balances reference error, distance stress, motion residual, and eccentricity within a small budget above it. We call this the \emph{reference-anchored layout}; it is part of our method (\cref{sec:method}). Yet even this layout deviates by more than 2\% of the axis in 58\% of the ERA5 time steps (up to 31\%) and reverses 22.4\% of clear motion readings, close to ST-MTM (24.9\%). The obstacle is the hierarchy. How large must the deviation be, which features cause it, and what does reducing it cost in merge-tree fidelity?

\subsection{Model}
Let the hierarchy be a rooted tree $T$ with leaves $1,\dots,n$. Leaf $i$ has a reference $q_i$, a width $w_i=cA_i$ with a scale $c$ that is fixed over time, and an eccentricity radius $h_i=\rho w_i/2$ with $\rho\in[0,1]$. Let $g>0$ be a minimum gap and $[a,a+L]$ the canvas. A \emph{layout} $(\pi,z,u)$ consists of a leaf order $\pi$, interval centres $z$, and anchors $u$ that satisfy
\begin{itemize}
\item[(H)] $\pi\in\Pi(T)$: the leaf set $S_v$ of every node $v$ is contiguous in $\pi$;
\item[(D)] $z_y-z_x\ge (w_x+w_y)/2+g$ for $\pi$-consecutive leaves $x,y$;
\item[(C)] $a+w_i/2\le z_i\le a+L-w_i/2$;
\item[(E)] $|u_i-z_i|\le h_i$.
\end{itemize}
Legal orders arise by permuting the blocks of children at every node, so $|\Pi(T)|=\prod_v\deg(v)!$. We distinguish three fidelities: \emph{position}, the deviation $u_i-q_i$; \emph{size}, widths proportional to support measure and comparable over time; and \emph{topology}, the merge levels shown by the rendered map (\cref{sec:topo}). TMTM preserves topology and absolute size but does not control position; ST-MTM preserves topology, reflects relative distances, and fits target width shares; the reference-anchored layout targets position under (H). Our analysis concerns position and topology at fixed size.

\section{A Positional Certificate}\label{sec:cert}

\subsection{Closed form and decomposition}

\begin{definition}
For an order $\pi$, $\tau(\pi)=\min_{z,u}\max_i|u_i-q_i|$ subject to (D), (C), (E). The \emph{certificate} is $\tau^*(T)=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(T)}\tau(\pi)$, and $\tau_\free$ is the same minimum over all $n!$ permutations. The \emph{hierarchy cost} is $H=\tau^*-\tau_\free$ and the \emph{space cost} is $\tau_\free$.
\end{definition}

$\tau^*$ is a property of the data and the hierarchy, not of an algorithm: no layout that keeps every subtree contiguous deviates less, and some layout attains it.

\begin{proposition}[Closed form]\label{prop:closed}
Number the leaves along $\pi$ and let $s_k=(w_k+w_{k+1})/2+g$, $S_{ij}=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}s_k$, $D_k=\sum_{m<k}w_m+w_k/2+(k-1)g$, and $E_k=\sum_{m>k}w_m+w_k/2+(n-k)g$. If $\sum_iw_i+(n-1)g\le L$, then
\begin{align*}
\tau(\pi)=\max\Big\{0,\ &\max_{i<j}\tfrac12\big(q_i-q_j+S_{ij}-h_i-h_j\big),\\
&\max_k\big(a+D_k-q_k-h_k\big),\ \max_k\big(q_k-h_k-a-L+E_k\big)\Big\}.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}
Eliminating the anchors gives box windows $|z_k-q_k|\le\tau+h_k$; a chain with lower-bounded separations is feasible iff its earliest placement stays inside every window, which expands to the formula (it agrees with a linear-programming solver to $2\cdot10^{-14}$). Two leaves in inverted reference order must share the overlap of their windows; the other terms handle the canvas ends.

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity, decomposition, witnesses]\label{prop:mono}
Flattening a node, i.e., attaching its children to its parent, enlarges $\Pi$ and never increases $\tau^*$; flattening all nodes yields $\tau_\free$. Hence $H\ge0$ is exactly the price of keeping every subtree contiguous. If $i,j\in S_v$, $k\notin S_v$, and $q_i<q_k<q_j$, then $\tau^*\ge\frac12\min(q_k-q_i,\,q_j-q_k)$.
\end{proposition}
The last statement holds because contiguity of $S_v$ forces $u_k$ outside $[u_i,u_j]$, so one of the three anchors must cross a reference that lies between the others. Such a \emph{witness triple} names the conflicting features. In \cref{fig:toy}, $(A,B;C)$ gives the bound 15, and the widths raise $\tau^*$ to 21. On the real datasets, the best witness explains a median of 92--94\% of $\tau^*$ in conflict frames, so a conflict is usually caused by one identifiable triple of features.

\subsection{Exact computation at display resolution}
Enumerating $\Pi(T)$ is exponential, so we compute $\tau^*$ at display resolution. Discretize the canvas into $N$ pixels with integer widths $W_i$ and gap $G$, and let $E_S(x)$ be the earliest start that is available after placing all leaves of subtree $S$ in some legal order, none starting before $x$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact dynamic program]\label{thm:dp}
For a leaf, $E_i(x)=\max(x,lo_i)+W_i+G$, or $\infty$ if $\max(x,lo_i)>hi_i$, where $[lo_i,hi_i]$ is the window of Proposition~\ref{prop:closed}. For a binary node, $E_S=\min(E_B\circ E_A,\ E_A\circ E_B)$; for an $m$-ary node, a subset recursion over its children. A deviation $\tau$ is feasible iff $E_{\rm root}(0)<\infty$. Bisection yields $\tau^*$ to a tolerance $\varepsilon$ in $O(nN\log(N/\varepsilon))$ time for binary hierarchies---pseudo-polynomial in $N$---and $O(2^m mN)$ per $m$-ary node.
\end{theorem}
Correctness rests on an earliest-completion argument: for a fixed order, greedy earliest placement minimizes every start, so a block influences the rest of the chain only through its earliest next start, and smaller is never worse. The $E_S$ are monotone arrays over $N+1$ starts, and composition is an array lookup.



\begin{proposition}[Certified lower bound]\label{prop:lb}
If widths and gap are integer multiples of the pixel size $\lambda$ and the canvas is grid-aligned, then $0\le\tau^*_{\rm disc}-\tau^*_{\rm cont}\le\lambda/2$. For real widths, rounding widths and gap \emph{down} while keeping the original slacks gives $\tau^*_{\rm cont}\ge\tau^*_{\rm disc,relax}-\lambda/2$.
\end{proposition}
Every continuous layout remains feasible for the relaxed discrete problem after shifting its starts to the pixel grid, which costs at most half a pixel; reported values also subtract the bisection tolerance. All values of the lower frontier $\Phi$ reported in \cref{sec:eval} use this certified bound. On 300 random hierarchies the program matches brute-force enumeration within 0.5 pixels; on all ERA5 and Ring frames, whose widths are not integer pixels, within 0.6 pixels at about 2\,ms per frame; and a 4096-leaf hierarchy on 16384 pixels takes 3.6\,s. The scalable part is the certificate; choosing a layout within the budget still enumerates candidate orders (\cref{sec:method}).

\subsection{A width-free certificate}
TMTM allocates one pixel per sample, and ST-MTM fits target widths. To bound such maps in their own width models, we keep only the order.

\begin{proposition}[Width-free certificate]\label{prop:pt}
Let $\tau_{\rm pt}(\pi)=\max\{0,\max_{i\text{ before }j}(q_i-q_j)/2\}$ and $\tau^*_{\rm pt}=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(T)}\tau_{\rm pt}(\pi)\le\tau^*$. For every 1-D map whose anchor order is hierarchy-consistent, with any width model, and for every non-decreasing calibration $c$ of its axis onto the reference axis, $\max_i|c(u_i)-q_i|\ge\tau^*_{\rm pt}$.
\end{proposition}
Two anchors in inverted order cannot both be closer than half their reference gap, whatever the calibration, and $\tau_{\rm pt}(\pi)$ is attained by $c(u_k)=\frac12(\max_{i\preceq k}q_i+\min_{j\succeq k}q_j)$. The width-free certificate therefore separates, for any existing map, the deviation that its hierarchy forces from the deviation that its layout chose (\cref{fig:teaser}a).

\section{The Price of Topology}\label{sec:topo}

\subsection{Measuring topology on a 1-D map}
Relaxing (H) reduces positional error but changes the displayed merge tree. Consider any function $\psi$ on the line (a \emph{filling}) with leaf anchors $u_i$. In a join tree, the 1-D merge level of leaves $i,j$ is $m_\psi(i,j)=\max_{[u_i,u_j]}\psi$, because the components of sublevel sets on a line are intervals (split trees are symmetric). We define the \emph{topological distortion}, the largest error of an anchor-to-anchor merge level,
\[\dtop=\max_{i\neq j}\,\big|m_\psi(i,j)-f(\lca_T(i,j))\big|.\]
When the local minima of the column are exactly the labelled anchors, with matching leaf values, $\dtop$ is the $\ell_\infty$-cophenetic distance between $T$ and the merge tree of the column, i.e., their labelled interleaving distance~\cite{Munch:2019:LINF}. It is zero exactly when the column shows the merge tree. Throughout, critical values are distinct (so $\gamma_v>0$), statements are given for join trees, and split trees follow by negating $f$.

\subsection{A lower bound for every filling}
\begin{theorem}[Topological price of non-contiguity]\label{thm:price}
If the leaf set of a non-root node $v$ is not contiguous in a 1-D map, then for every filling $\psi$, $\dtop\ge\gamma_v/2$ with $\gamma_v=f(p(v))-f(v)$, where $p(v)$ is the parent of $v$. The factor $1/2$ is tight.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $i,j\in S_v$ and $k\notin S_v$ with $u_k$ between $u_i$ and $u_j$. Interval inclusion gives $m_\psi(i,k)\le m_\psi(i,j)$, whereas $m_T(i,j)\le f(v)$ and $m_T(i,k)\ge f(p(v))$. Hence $\gamma_v\le m_T(i,k)-m_T(i,j)=[m_T(i,k)-m_\psi(i,k)]+[m_\psi(i,k)-m_\psi(i,j)]+[m_\psi(i,j)-m_T(i,j)]\le2\dtop$. For tightness, take $T=((i,j)_v,k)$ with $f(k)\le f(v)+\gamma_v/2$, anchors in order $i,k,j$, and both barriers at $f(v)+\gamma_v/2$.
\end{proof}
The theorem constrains the map, not the algorithm: weak merges (small $\gamma_v$) are cheap to break for any method, strong merges expensive.

\subsection{The optimal filling}
For a fixed order, the best \emph{value-preserving} filling, which shows every extremum at its true value, has a closed form. Number the leaves along the map, keep the true leaf values at the anchors, and let $P_{ij}=f(\lca(i,j))$ for $i<j$, $C_k=\min_{i\le k<j}P_{ij}$, and $\ell_k=\max(f(k),f(k+1))$.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal filling]\label{prop:fill}
Over value-preserving fillings, $\inf_\psi\dtop=\delta^*(\pi)=\max\{0,\ \max_k(\ell_k-C_k),\ \max_{i<j}\frac12(P_{ij}-\max_{i\le k<j}C_k)\}$. It is attained by the \emph{barrier filling}, whose maximum between anchors $k$ and $k+1$ is $b_k=C_k+\delta^*(\pi)$; if every anchor must remain a strict minimum, it is approached arbitrarily closely by raising barriers with $b_k=\ell_k$ by any $\varepsilon>0$. Moreover, $\delta^*(\pi)=0$ iff $\pi\in\Pi(T)$.
\end{proposition}
Any filling acts on merge levels only through its barrier heights $b_k$ between consecutive anchors, with $m_\psi(i,j)=\max_{i\le k<j}b_k$. The upper constraints $b_k\le P_{ij}+\delta$ collapse to $b_k\le C_k+\delta$, and raising a barrier only helps the lower constraints, so $b_k=C_k+\delta$ is optimal; feasibility then reduces to the two inequalities in the formula. The term $\ell_k-C_k$ reveals a second mechanism next to Theorem~\ref{thm:price}: a shallow extremum placed between two leaves that merge deeply raises their 1-D merge level at least to its own value, whatever value-preserving filling is used. If a filling may also move extremum values, which the labelled interleaving distance permits, this term halves to $\frac12(\ell_k-C_k)$; we keep extrema at their true values because readers read depths from them. The pair term has the structure of $\ell_\infty$ fitting by ultrametrics~\cite{Farach:1995:RM,Chepoi:2000:LIA}, here constrained to the ultrametrics that a line with fixed leaf values can realize. The LCA-path filling of existing maps is not optimal; on our data it pays up to twice $\delta^*$. The barrier filling changes a rendered column only between anchors, by clipping each segment to $b_k$ and raising its existing peak to $b_k$ if necessary, so no new extrema appear; our renderer raises barriers by about $10^{-6}$ relative to the field values above adjacent leaf values, so every anchor stays a strict extremum and every feature remains visible.

\subsection{The position--topology frontier}
\begin{corollary}[Certified lower frontier]\label{cor:frontier}
Let $\Phi_T(\delta)$ be $\tau^*$ of $T$ with every node of $\gamma_v\le2\delta$ flattened. Every layout satisfying (D), (C), (E), with any filling, obeys $\max_i|u_i-q_i|\ge\Phi_T(\dtop)$.
\end{corollary}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:price}, every non-contiguous node of a layout with distortion $\dtop$ has $\gamma_v\le2\dtop$, so the layout is legal for the flattened tree, whose certificate is $\Phi_T(\dtop)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:mono}). No 1-D map can simultaneously keep the positional error below $\Phi_T(\delta)$ and the topological distortion at or below $\delta$. $\Phi_T$ is a step function with breakpoints at $\gamma_v/2$, and we evaluate it per frame with the certified lower bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:lb}. It scales to large hierarchies but is in general not attainable. For small trees, the attainable frontier itself can be computed exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Exact position--topology frontier]\label{thm:exact}
A value-preserving map with order $\pi$ has $\max_i|u_i-q_i|\ge\tau(\pi)$ and $\dtop\ge\delta^*(\pi)$, and both bounds are attained, or approached arbitrarily closely, simultaneously. Hence the achievable pairs over all value-preserving 1-D maps have the lower envelope $F_T(\delta)=\min\{\tau(\pi):\pi\in S_n,\ \delta^*(\pi)\le\delta\}$, with $F_T(0)=\tau^*$, $F_T(\infty)=\tau_\free$, and $\Phi_T\le F_T$.
\end{theorem}
Positions enter only through $\tau(\pi)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:closed}) and merge levels only through $\delta^*(\pi)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:fill}). The barrier filling prescribes values only between anchors, which remain separated by the gap $g$, so the position-optimal layout and the optimal filling can be combined. We evaluate $F_T$ by enumerating all $n!$ orders with both closed forms, which covers every frame in our data ($n\le11$).

\section{Certificate-Driven Merge Tree Maps}\label{sec:method}

The theory suggests keeping the merge tree where this is cheap and relaxing it only where the certificate shows a conflict (\cref{alg:pipeline}; \cref{fig:pipeline} traces one time step).

\textbf{Reference and gate.} The reference direction $d$ is the principal direction of tracked extremum displacements, and $q_i=\langle R_i,d\rangle$ is scaled to the axis; the choice is independent of the data axes and captures 0.74 of the ERA5 motion (longitude: 0.69). For every time step we compute $\tau^*$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}) and $\tau_\free$, and relax only if $H$ exceeds the legibility constant $\theta=2\%$ of the axis.

\textbf{Threshold and prune.} We flatten all nodes with $\gamma_v\le\kappa$, then undo flattenings, strongest first, whose removal does not raise $\tau^*$ by more than half a pixel. Flattening jointly and pruning afterwards handles plateaus on which only a set of flattenings lowers $\tau^*$, where a greedy one-node policy stalls (ERA5 frame~9 needs two). The resulting topological cost is computed exactly by Proposition~\ref{prop:fill}; the policy has no approximation guarantee (\cref{sec:rq3} compares it with the exact frontier).

\textbf{Layout.} Within the budget $B_t=\tau^*(T')+\beta$ of the possibly relaxed tree $T'$, one quadratic program per candidate order ($\tau(\pi)\le B_t$) minimizes reference error, distance stress, motion residual (each tracked anchor should move like its reference), and eccentricity, with weights 4, 1, 0.5, and 0.1, each normalized by its number of terms, subject to (D), (C), (E), and $|u_i-q_i|\le B_t$.

\textbf{Rendering and disclosure.} Relaxed columns are rendered with the barrier filling, so the displayed topological distortion is exactly $\delta^*(\pi)$. The disclosure answers three questions. \emph{Can I read positions here?} A strip shows the certificate per time step, split into space and hierarchy cost, with $\theta$ as a reference line (\cref{fig:teaser}b). \emph{Which features are affected?} In conflict steps the best witness triple is drawn on the map: a bar joins two leaves of one subtree and a ring marks the leaf that lies between them in the reference (\cref{fig:witness}a); features can additionally be flagged by their deviation. \emph{What was given up?} Relaxed steps are marked, and a second strip shows $\delta^*$ in data units (\cref{fig:teaser}c). We use strips because the map already uses colour for the field and position for the reference; per-feature error ticks were illegible (\cref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Constants.} All constants are canvas-relative and shared by all datasets: the width scale maps the domain area to half the axis, $g=L/240$, $\beta=L/120$, $\rho=0.5$, and $\theta=2\%$ (8 pixels on a 400-pixel map, five times the gap; \cref{sec:rq1} reports 1\% and 5\%). The merge-gap threshold $\kappa$ is the user's operating point on the trade-off; it bounds the gaps of flattened merges, not the distortion $\delta^*$, which the strips report. We report $\kappa\in\{2\%,20\%,\infty\}$ of the value range and use 20\% in figures.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Certificate-driven merge tree map. Constants: $\theta=2\%$ of the axis; canvas-relative $g,\beta,\rho$; budget $\kappa$.}\label{alg:pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require merge trees $T_t$ with leaf extrema $R_i$ and supports $A_i$; feature tracks
\State $d\gets$ principal direction of tracked extremum displacements;\ $q_i\gets\langle R_i,d\rangle$ scaled to the axis
\For{$t=1,\dots,T$}
  \State $\tau^*\gets$ DP of Theorem~\ref{thm:dp} on $T_t$;\ \ $\tau_\free\gets\min_{\pi\in S_n}\tau(\pi)$;\ \ $H_t\gets\tau^*-\tau_\free$
  \State $T'\gets T_t$
  \If{$H_t>\theta$} \Comment{threshold and prune}
    \State $W\gets\{v:\gamma_v\le\kappa\}$
    \For{$v\in W$ by decreasing $\gamma_v$}
      \If{$\tau^*(\mathrm{flat}(T_t,W\setminus\{v\}))\le\tau^*(\mathrm{flat}(T_t,W))+$ half a pixel}
        \State $W\gets W\setminus\{v\}$
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State $T'\gets\mathrm{flat}(T_t,W)$
  \EndIf
  \State $B_t\gets\tau^*(T')+\beta$ \Comment{error budget}
  \State $(\pi,z,u)\gets$ over $\pi\in\Pi(T')$ with $\tau(\pi)\le B_t$, minimize distance stress, reference error, eccentricity, and motion residual w.r.t.\ $t-1$ subject to (D)(C)(E) and $|u_i-q_i|\le B_t$ \Comment{one QP per order}
  \State fill column $t$ along leaf arcs and LCA paths; clip barriers to $C_k+\delta^*(\pi)$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:fill})
  \State strips: $\tau^*$, $H_t$, $\max_i|u_i-q_i|$, $\delta^*(\pi)$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{RQ1: How common are hierarchy conflicts?}\label{sec:rq1}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_rq1}

Under the automatic direction, $H>\theta$ in 58\%, 60\%, and 63\% of the time steps of ERA5 1999/2000, ERA5 2013/14, and wildfire, with maxima of 31\%, 38\%, and 19\% of the axis (\cref{fig:rq1}). The space cost never exceeds 5\%: some order could place the features almost exactly, and the hierarchy prevents it. The share is 60--67\% for $\theta=1\%$ and 52--57\% for $\theta=5\%$, 23--77\% across eight fixed directions, 50--69\% for the width-free certificate on all five datasets, 47--86\% for ERA5 smoothing of 150--350\,km, and 57--73\% for fire leaf caps of 8--12. $\tau_\free$ is exact (all $n!$ orders enumerated).

\textbf{Simplification reduces conflicts by deleting features.} Persistence simplification at 1\% and 2\% of the value range removes 30\% and 40\% of the ERA5 leaf instances (35/43\% in 2013/14, 22/35\% for fire), yet conflicts remain in 42\% and 34\% of the ERA5 frames (27/18\%; 57/33\%); only at 5\%, after more than half of the features are gone, do they fall to 7--15\%. Flattening keeps every feature and pays in merge levels instead.

\textbf{What existing maps could avoid.} Under their best monotone calibration, TMTM and ST-MTM deviate from the reference by 24--29\% of the axis on average on real data, of which 6--8\% is unavoidable for any merge-tree-preserving order (\cref{fig:teaser}a).

\subsection{RQ2: What do conflicts do to motion readings?}\label{sec:rq2}
We simulate how a reader judges approach or separation of two tracked features from their vertical distance. Over a window of $k=2$ steps (24\,h for ERA5, four days for fire), the proxy reads \emph{approach} or \emph{separation} when the map distance changes by more than 2\% of the method's occupied anchor range, and \emph{no change} otherwise; the truth is the change of the 2-D extremum distance beyond 2\% of the domain diagonal. On clear cases, a \emph{reversal} reads the opposite and a \emph{miss} reads no change; the \emph{error} is their sum. Method differences use a circular moving-block bootstrap over time (blocks of $k+2$ steps).

\textbf{Existing maps and exact projection.} Existing maps reverse 21--34\% of clear readings on real data (\cref{tab:proxy}), below but near the permutation null (36--40\%). Even exact projection reverses 13--14\% on pressure and 5\% on fire, because one direction cannot show orthogonal motion; it is a reference point, not a bound (R at $\kappa=\infty$ on fire and ST-MTM on Ring fall below it). On ERA5, A lies 10 points above it, consistent with the hierarchy cost of RQ1.

\textbf{Relaxation as a trade-off.} At $\kappa=2\%$ (largest merge-level error below 2\% of the value range), reversals on pressure stay at the level of A and ST-MTM. At $\kappa=20\%$ they drop to 15--16\% on pressure and 6\% on fire, below ST-MTM by 10.2, 12.2, and 15.2 points (95\% intervals $[-16.4,-4.9]$, $[-19.7,-5.2]$, $[-25.8,-3.8]$; fire has 8 blocks and is descriptive), and the error drops by 27.8 and 22.5 points on pressure ($[-34.4,-20.5]$, $[-34.7,-10.9]$); on fire it drops by a non-significant 4.5 points as reversals turn into misses. The price is a largest merge-level error of 20--33\% of the value range, 3--4\% on average. At $\kappa=\infty$, R matches exact projection at a largest merge-level error of 51--65\%. On Ring, where ST-MTM uses its published parameters, it reverses less (7.3\% vs 10.5\%, n.s.) but misses far more (error 35\% vs 13\%).

\textbf{Robustness.} \emph{Direction:} relaxing along fixed $x$ instead of the fitted direction raises R's reversals at $\kappa=20\%$ to 19\%, 16\%, and 12\%, still 6--12 points below ST-MTM. \emph{ST-MTM parameters:} over a grid spanning all four published presets (two weightings, $r\in\{0.65,0.85,0.95\}$, $\lambda\in\{0.05,0.5,1.5\}$), low-reversal settings freeze the layout (up to 84\% misses), and the best error is 48\%, 54\%, 34\%, and 32\% on ERA5, ERA5-14, fire, and Ring, against 24\%, 32\%, 31\%, and 13\% for R. \emph{Reader settings:} over three thresholds at $k=2$ and windows of 1, 4, and 8 steps, R reverses less than ST-MTM in 17 of 18 settings on real data, significantly in 12 (exploratory; the exception is the 8-step window on ERA5-14). \emph{Feature extraction:} on pressure, R has the lowest reversal and error rates among the compared maps at every smoothing scale tested; on fire with a leaf cap of 8, exact projection itself reverses 23\% and relaxation no longer lowers reversals (error 33\% vs 35\%). The proxy measures what the geometry of a map supports, not human performance.

\textbf{Does the certificate locate errors?} A frame-level flag ($H>\theta$) marks most clear cases and does not predict where TMTM or ST-MTM reverse. A per-feature flag, raised when either feature deviates by more than $\theta$ under the best monotone calibration (Proposition~\ref{prop:pt}) and thus applicable to any map, catches 86--89\% of the reversals of A on real data; flagged cases are 1.6--3.2 times as likely to be reversed, but the flag marks 71--82\% of clear cases, and unflagged cases still reverse in 6--15\%. For R at $\kappa=20\%$ it catches 24--57\% of the remaining reversals. It is a diagnostic, not a guarantee.

\textbf{Cost.} At $\kappa=2\%$, relaxation lowers the mean reference error by 11--13\% on pressure at 1.4--2.1\,hPa of merge-level error (2.5--2.7\,hPa with the LCA filling), but by 45\% on fire; at $\kappa=20\%$, by 59--74\% on pressure at 28--32\,hPa. Fire conflicts stem from weak merges between neighbouring hot spots, pressure conflicts from strong saddles and shallow lows.

\textbf{Optimality on relaxed frames.} We test only frames that the method relaxes. For the order that Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline} actually selects, $(\tau(\pi),\delta^*(\pi))$ lies on the exact frontier within half a pixel in all 54 relaxed frames at $\kappa=2\%$, in 152 of 155 at $\kappa=20\%$, and in 178 of 182 at $\kappa=\infty$ (four datasets); it is never more than 0.6\% of the axis above it. As a diagnostic of the relaxation policy alone, we also compare the $\tau$-optimal orders of the hierarchies produced by three policies. Threshold-and-prune is on the frontier in every relaxed frame. A greedy policy that flattens one node at a time stalls on plateaus: at $\kappa=\infty$ it resolves only 130 of 182 conflicts (71\%) and leaves the frontier in 8 of its 145 relaxed frames. Flattening all admissible nodes without pruning resolves the same conflicts as our policy but changes the merge tree in 2.1 times as many frames at $\kappa=2\%$ (115 vs 54). The aggregate points in \cref{fig:frontier} lie above $F$ because $\kappa$ selects nodes by $\gamma_v$, whereas the exact cost $\delta^*$ also contains the leaf-height term, so individual frames spend less than the global threshold.

\textbf{Tightness of the certified bound.} $F-\Phi$ averages 0.3--2.3\% of the axis over the breakpoints of $\Phi$, is within half a pixel at 29--36\% of them, and reaches 25\% in a few frames; $\Phi$ ignores the leaf-height term. The optimal filling halves $\dtop$ where only merge gaps matter (Ring, Gaussians) but saves little on pressure at large $\kappa$, again because of shallow lows.

\textbf{Hidden costs} (\cref{tab:hidden}). On the real datasets, tracked pairs swap vertical order in 1--4\% of consecutive steps on R, against 7--33\% on TMTM and ST-MTM (on Ring, ST-MTM swaps fewer); displacement-based stability is mixed, as R's anchors follow the lows. R preserves 2-D distances worse than ST-MTM on pressure (nearest-neighbour preservation 0.49 vs 0.78 on ERA5) and about as well as exact projection, so much of this cost comes from reading one direction; ST-MTM is preferable when 2-D neighbourhoods matter more. At $\kappa=20\%$ the barrier filling changes 4--8\% of the pixels, all in relaxed columns.

\subsection{RQ4: Does the certificate transfer?}\label{sec:rq4}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{dendrogram_demo}
\caption{1024 wildfire cells (rows) over two months (columns), ordered by an optimal-leaf-ordered dendrogram of their fire time series (left) and by geography (right); side panels show each row's displacement from its geographic position in \% of the display height. OLO displaces cells by up to 88\%; the certificate proves that no dendrogram-consistent order can do better than 67\% (dashed).}
\label{fig:dendro}

Dense pixel displays often order rows by a dendrogram. We ordered 1024 active 10-km fire cells, one row each, by agglomerative clustering of their fire time series, the hierarchy behind hierarchical pixel orderings~\cite{Franke:2021:1DP}, with each cell's position along the principal geographic axis, scaled linearly, as reference. Any order consistent with this dendrogram must displace some cell by at least 67\% of the display height (78--82\% with a rank-based reference), whereas geographic order stays within 21\%. The hierarchy cost is 47--48\% for average, complete, and Ward linkage, versus 1.4\% for a spatial Ward and 32.5\% for a spatial single-linkage dendrogram. Optimal leaf ordering puts cells up to 88\% (median 14\%) away from geography (\cref{fig:dendro}). Before anyone reads geography off such a display, the certificate shows that row position cannot approximate location here, and a linked map or a spatially constrained hierarchy is needed. The computation takes 0.1\,s.

\textbf{Runtime.} The certificate takes about 2\,ms per merge-tree frame at 1024 pixels and 3.6\,s for 4096 leaves on 16384 pixels; the exact frontier, which enumerates $n!$ orders, takes minutes per dataset and is dominated by the few frames with 10 or 11 leaves.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Implications.} Keep the hierarchy by default and disclose the certified deviation, so readers know when positions can be trusted; when position matters, relax explicitly, choose $\kappa$ from the trade-off, and display the exact topological cost; and when proposing layouts, report error relative to the certificate and to exact projection, separating what a method chose, what the hierarchy forced, and what one direction cannot show.

\textbf{Limits of the theory.} The certificate concerns one reference coordinate, which cannot encode orthogonal motion; radial or path-based references need their own evaluation. The optimal filling and the exact frontier assume value-preserving fillings, and with strict extrema the optimum is an infimum. The dynamic program is pseudo-polynomial in the resolution and exponential in node degree; whether $\tau^*$ is NP-hard for unbounded degree is open. The exact frontier enumerates $n!$ orders, which our preprocessing keeps feasible; the scalable bound $\Phi$ ignores the leaf-height term. The relaxation has no approximation guarantee, and $\kappa$ selects nodes by merge gap rather than by exact cost.

\textbf{Limits of the evaluation.} The reader proxy is geometric, and we make no claim about human performance. The reference direction is fitted on the tracks that define the truth; relaxing along a fixed direction keeps most, but not all, of the gain. ST-MTM was reimplemented from its description; its published Ring parameters attain the lowest reversal rate of our grid, but we could not compare with an original implementation. Our ERA5 fields come from a public mirror and reproduce the features of the original files without being byte-identical. For ERA5 2013/14, 150\,km smoothing exceeds the 12 leaves per frame that exhaustive evaluation allows, and the fire background term was not varied.

\textbf{Negative results.} Automatic radial reference centres followed tracking artefacts, a stability-priority relaxation policy did not generalize, and per-feature error ticks were illegible at full map scale, which is why we draw witness triples only in conflict steps.

\section{Conclusion}
We asked how faithful a hierarchy-constrained 1-D layout can be. The positional certificate answers this per time step, exactly and at display resolution, and names the features responsible. The topological price of breaking contiguity, the optimal filling, and the exact frontier describe what it costs to do better. Across two domains, hierarchy conflicts are frequent, reverse motion readings, and can be resolved at a data-dependent and exactly reported topological cost. We hope that certificates of this kind become part of how hierarchy-constrained visualizations are designed and evaluated.
